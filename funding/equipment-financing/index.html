Equipment financing

Compare borrowing for equipment with leasing its use.

The key idea

The full cost includes ownership, upkeep, and end-of-term obligations—not just the payment.

What it is

Equipment financing helps acquire eligible business equipment. A loan typically finances a purchase; a lease provides use under its agreement. Ownership, buyout rights, and obligations differ. Do not assume every lease leads to ownership.

How it works

A provider reviews the equipment and business, then specifies the amount financed, payment schedule, security, and any end-of-term requirements. The equipment may secure the obligation. Delivery, installation, maintenance, insurance, and taxes can sit outside the advertised payment.

Illustration only—not an offer

Before financing a kitchen appliance, a restaurant compares the equipment quote with installation, electrical work, maintenance, and a quieter-than-expected month.

When it may fit

A business with a defined asset need and a realistic plan to use it may compare financing with cash purchase, leasing, rental, or used equipment. Consider how long the asset should remain useful and whether demand justifies it.

What providers may review

Providers may examine cash flow, credit, time operating, equipment age and resale value, a vendor quote, down payment or owner contribution, and guarantees. Specialized or rapidly obsolete assets can be assessed differently from readily resold equipment.

Costs, repayment & risks

Compare interest, fees, payment frequency, total payments, insurance, and buyout or return conditions. The debt can outlast the equipment’s usefulness. A missed obligation can put the equipment or other pledged assets at risk. Confirm whether paying early changes the total cost.

Questions to ask

  • Who owns the equipment during and after the agreement?
  • What delivery, maintenance, insurance, and tax costs are excluded?
  • What happens if the equipment fails or becomes obsolete?
  • Is there a final buyout, residual payment, or return charge?

Compare any proposal with the cost guide and cash-flow walkthrough before deciding.
