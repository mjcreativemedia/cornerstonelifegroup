Real estate investment financing

Understand property-backed financing and the source of repayment.

The key idea

The property’s income or exit plan must support the obligation—not just its projected value.

What it is

Real estate investment financing supports property held for rental income, resale, or another investment purpose. It differs from financing a home someone intends to occupy. A purchase, a stabilized rental, and a renovation can require different structures.

How it works

The lender generally evaluates the borrower and property, secures the financing against the property, and sets payment and maturity terms. Depending on the transaction, repayment may rely on rents, business cash flow, sale proceeds, or refinancing. A refinance is a new credit decision, not a guaranteed exit.

Illustration only—not an offer

For a hypothetical rental, compare expected rent with vacancy, repairs, taxes, insurance, and debt payments—not with the debt payment alone.

When it may fit

Investors with a defined property strategy, documented costs, and reserves may compare property financing with additional equity or a smaller acquisition. It can be unsuitable when the plan relies on uninterrupted occupancy or an optimistic sale price.

What providers may review

Common review areas include property value and condition, rents and leases, expenses, borrower credit and liquidity, experience, existing liens, and owner equity. LTV—loan-to-value—compares the loan with the property valuation; it is not the same as cash needed at closing.

Costs, repayment & risks

Budget for interest, lender and closing fees, valuation, insurance, taxes, and reserves. Some agreements have variable rates, prepayment charges, or a large balance due at maturity. Vacancy, repairs, falling values, and refinancing changes can reduce the margin for repayment. Default can put the property at risk.

Questions to ask

  • What valuation and income assumptions support the amount?
  • Is the loan fully repaid over time, or is a balloon balance due?
  • What happens if rent or sale proceeds fall short?
  • What cash contribution, reserves, and guarantees are required?

Compare any proposal with the cost guide and cash-flow walkthrough before deciding.
