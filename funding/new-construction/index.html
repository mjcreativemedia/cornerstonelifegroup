New construction financing

Follow how staged funding, project risk, and the final repayment plan fit together.

Educational overview. This is not a confirmed Cornerstone partner offering. Ask Carlos about current availability; providers determine eligibility and terms.

The key idea

Money is released in stages, while delays can keep adding cost.

What it is

New construction financing helps fund a building project rather than a completed asset. Land acquisition, site work, and construction may be handled differently in the agreement. The final property value depends on successful completion.

How it works

A project budget and draw schedule guide staged disbursements. Inspections and evidence of completed work can precede releases. The loan may end with a sale, repayment from other funds, or a separate permanent loan. Do not assume that construction financing automatically converts to long-term financing.

Illustration only—not an offer

A builder compares an on-schedule budget with a delayed completion. Extra interest and insurance must be funded even if no new work is being completed.

When it may fit

A borrower with a feasible project, prepared team, documented plans, and enough liquidity for gaps and contingencies may explore it. A project without approvals, reliable costs, or a supported exit needs more planning first.

What providers may review

Providers may review land ownership and liens, zoning and permits, plans, budget, builder experience, contracts, feasibility, borrower finances, equity, and projected completed value. Requirements are project- and provider-specific; collateral value is created as work progresses.

Costs, repayment & risks

Consider interest, commitment and draw fees, inspections, insurance, extensions, and the costs of unused or delayed funds under the contract. Overruns, material changes, weather, incomplete work, and an unavailable replacement loan to repay the construction balance can create a funding gap. The property and any guarantees can be at risk.

Questions to ask

  • Which conditions must be met before each draw?
  • Who covers an overrun or an amount withheld from a draw?
  • What is the repayment plan if completion or sale is late?
  • Is permanent financing committed separately, and on what conditions?

Compare any proposal with the cost guide and cash-flow walkthrough before deciding.
