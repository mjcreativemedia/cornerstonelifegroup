Working capital funding

Understand the operating need before choosing a financing product.

The key idea

Working capital describes a business need—not one specific funding product.

What it is

Working capital is commonly current assets minus current liabilities: resources expected to turn into cash within a year, less obligations due within that period. In financing conversations it often means money for everyday operations: payroll, supplies, inventory, or a gap before customers pay. A credit line, loan, or other arrangement can serve that purpose but work very differently.

How it works

Map when customer cash is expected and when bills are due. Then compare a funding structure with that operating cycle. A short gap differs from a long-term investment. Funding adds its own payment schedule rather than removing the original expense.

Illustration only—not an offer

A supplier needs payment before a customer invoice is due. Put both dates, a possible late payment, and financing collections on one calendar.

When it may fit

A temporary, explainable timing gap may support a different decision from recurring losses. Consider collecting invoices sooner, changing supplier terms, reducing the order, or waiting alongside borrowing. Those alternatives can also have business costs or tradeoffs.

What providers may review

Providers may review cash receipts, expenses, operating history, debt commitments, industry, and credit or collateral depending on the product. Current records help distinguish earned revenue from projections and overdue receivables from collected cash.

Costs, repayment & risks

Interest, fees, payment frequency, and guarantees depend on the underlying product. Borrowing to cover each month’s ordinary shortfall can make the next shortfall larger. Test a slower-sales period and include taxes, owner withdrawals, and existing payments in the forecast.

Questions to ask

  • What exactly creates the cash gap, and when should it close?
  • Will collections arrive before funding payments begin?
  • How much cash remains after all obligations?
  • Is there a smaller or non-borrowing way to meet the need?

Compare any proposal with the cost guide and cash-flow walkthrough before deciding.
