Secured business lines of credit

Borrow as needed against an approved limit, with business assets at risk.

The key idea

A secured line offers flexibility, but collateral and renewal terms matter as much as the limit.

What it is

A secured business line of credit is a borrowing facility backed by specified assets. A lien is the lender’s legal claim against collateral. “Secured” describes that support; it does not mean approval is certain or repayment is risk-free. In these materials, SLOC means a secured business line of credit.

How it works

An approved business can request draws up to its available limit. A revolving line generally restores borrowing room as principal is repaid, subject to the agreement. Some facilities base availability on eligible receivables or inventory; availability can change as the collateral changes.

Illustration only—not an offer

A business expecting an invoice payment next month considers a temporary draw for supplies. It still needs to check the customer’s payment risk and the line’s repayment date.

When it may fit

A business with recurring timing gaps and an identifiable repayment source may find a line useful. It can be a poor fit for permanent operating losses or a project that needs years to produce cash. Compare an unsecured line or a term loan as well; each has its own requirements.

What providers may review

Providers may assess operating history, revenue, cash flow, existing debt, credit, and the ownership, value, and quality of proposed collateral. Ask whether personal guarantees, financial reporting, inspections, or periodic renewal are required. No single minimum applies to every provider.

Costs, repayment & risks

Interest, draw or annual fees, collateral reviews, and other charges may apply. Rates can be variable. Ask when interest and principal are due and whether a balance becomes payable at maturity. Default—failing to meet the agreement—can put pledged assets at risk; renewal or further draws may not be available.

Questions to ask

  • Which assets are pledged, and is the lien limited or broad?
  • How is the available limit calculated and reviewed?
  • Can the provider reduce, freeze, or decline to renew the line?
  • What must be repaid at maturity, and what fees and guarantees apply?

Compare any proposal with the cost guide and cash-flow walkthrough before deciding.
