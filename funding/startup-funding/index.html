Startup funding

Compare borrowing, owner funds, and outside investment before opening.

Educational overview. This is not a confirmed Cornerstone partner offering. Ask Carlos about current availability; providers determine eligibility and terms.

The key idea

Without operating history, assumptions and owner risk deserve extra attention.

What it is

Startup funding supports a new business before or during its early operations. It is a stage of business, not one loan product. Potential paths include owner funds, investors, and credit where available; grants are limited to specific programs and are not a universal source of startup cash.

How it works

Build a startup budget, separate one-time setup expenses from continuing costs, and estimate when customer cash might arrive. Debt requires repayment; equity investment gives someone an ownership interest and may change control. Self-funding concentrates risk in the owner’s resources.

Illustration only—not an offer

A prospective owner separates equipment and deposits from rent, payroll, and supplies needed before sales stabilize. The opening-day budget alone does not show the whole funding need.

When it may fit

A new venture with tested demand, realistic costs, and a way to withstand slower sales may compare several paths. A smaller launch or more validation may be preferable if borrowing would depend on immediate, unproven revenue.

What providers may review

Providers or investors may examine the business plan, owner credit or contribution, relevant experience, existing resources, forecasts, and collateral or guarantees. Forecasts must be labeled as forecasts. A startup is not automatically eligible for a program because it is described online.

Costs, repayment & risks

Borrowing costs and schedules vary; repayment can start before the business is profitable. Guarantees can expose the owner personally. Investor funding trades ownership and potentially decision rights for capital. Review documentation with appropriate professional help, and avoid “guaranteed startup funding” claims.

Questions to ask

  • Which figures are actual results and which are assumptions?
  • What happens if opening or sales are delayed?
  • What personal assets or ownership rights are at stake?
  • Would a smaller launch reduce the funding need?

Compare any proposal with the cost guide and cash-flow walkthrough before deciding.
