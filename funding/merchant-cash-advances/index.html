Merchant cash advances

Understand receipts-based financing, factor pricing, and collection pressure.

The key idea

An advance is not free cash, and a factor rate is not an APR.

What it is

A merchant cash advance (MCA) generally provides upfront money in exchange for an agreed amount of future business receipts. Agreements are often structured as purchases of receivables rather than traditional loans. Read the actual contract; the label alone does not establish the economics or legal treatment.

How it works

Collections may be a share of card sales or recurring bank withdrawals. A reconciliation process may adjust collections to actual receipts if the contract provides for it. Do not assume a fixed withdrawal automatically falls when sales slow.

Illustration only—not an offer

A purely illustrative $10,000 advance at a 1.25 factor implies $12,500 before other charges. That arithmetic does not make the cost a 25% annual percentage rate (APR); collection timing still matters.

When it may fit

A business evaluating receipts-based financing needs dependable receipts and enough cash after collections to operate. Compare alternatives before using an advance for an uncertain startup, a long construction project, or a continuing loss. Access to funding is not the same as affordable funding.

What providers may review

Providers may examine transaction history, bank statements, sales volatility, existing advances, industry, operating history, and owner credit or guarantees. Approval criteria differ. Ask what data and account access would be needed and who receives them.

Costs, repayment & risks

Factor pricing states a multiplier, not an annualized interest rate. Additional fees and the timing of collections affect the true burden. Frequent withdrawals can crowd out payroll or rent. Ask about reconciliation, default terms, early payoff, and restrictions on other financing; obligations and guarantees depend on the agreement.

Questions to ask

  • What is the net cash received and complete purchased-receipts obligation?
  • Is collection fixed or sales-based, and how is reconciliation requested?
  • Does early payoff reduce the obligation?
  • What happens if receipts fall or another payment is missed?

Compare any proposal with the cost guide and cash-flow walkthrough before deciding.
