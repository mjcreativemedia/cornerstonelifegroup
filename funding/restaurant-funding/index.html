Restaurant funding

Match the financing structure to your operation, seasonality, and margins.

The key idea

A restaurant’s daily sales do not equal the cash it can safely commit to payments.

What it is

Restaurant funding describes financing for an industry, not a single product. A new opening, kitchen equipment purchase, seasonal inventory order, and second location have different needs. Available structures depend on the provider and the business.

How it works

Start with the specific expense and the timing of the benefit. Equipment borrowing or leasing, an operating credit line, or a term loan can have different cash demands. An MCA ties collections to receipts under its contract and needs separate cost scrutiny. This is a comparison framework, not a list of Cornerstone programs.

Illustration only—not an offer

A busy restaurant tests a lower-sales month after food, labor, rent, and existing debt. It compares the new payment with cash remaining, not with gross receipts.

When it may fit

An established restaurant with documented cash flow may approach a purchase differently from a pre-opening location. Compare postponing a nonessential remodel, renting equipment, or phasing expansion alongside taking on a new obligation.

What providers may review

Providers may review bank and processor records, operating history, food and labor costs, lease terms, existing obligations, credit, experience, owner investment, and permits or opening plans where relevant. No universal restaurant approval threshold is stated here.

Costs, repayment & risks

Compare total fees and repayment frequency with payroll, rent, supplier bills, delivery-platform charges, and quieter periods. Spoilage, repairs, seasonality, and a delayed opening can erode expected cash. Guarantees and collateral requirements vary. Frequent collections can put pressure on a business even when sales look healthy.

Questions to ask

  • Is the need temporary cash flow, equipment, opening costs, or expansion?
  • Does the schedule still fit a slower trading period?
  • How do all current financing payments interact?
  • What obligations continue if the location closes or changes hands?

Compare any proposal with the cost guide and cash-flow walkthrough before deciding.
